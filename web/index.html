The Problem

Every trading agent answers the wrong question.

"Which trade should I take?" is a commodity question — signal generation is well-trodden ground. The real failure mode in automated trading isn't a bad signal. It's the absence of governance: full-size positions held through choppy or adverse regimes, with no systematic kill-switch. Bitget's Playbook ecosystem already supplies strategies. What's missing is a layer that decides whether to trust them right now.

Signal Generators

Pick the trade. Answer "what should I buy?" — no opinion on whether trading should happen at all.

Signal Filters & Benchmarks

Score or rank signals after the fact. Improve signal quality, but still defer to the agent on capital and timing.

Aegis — Governance Layer

Sits above all of it. Decides whether to trade, which Playbooks are trusted, and how much capital to risk — before any signal matters.

Key Features

A governance layer, not a guess.

◆

Regime Detection

Classifies markets into four regimes from live Agent Hub signals and adjusts risk policy before any trade is considered.

◆

Playbook Governance

Plugs into existing Bitget GetAgent Playbooks unmodified — Aegis decides which are trusted, it never replaces a strategy.

◆

Risk Budgeting

Sets a global risk budget and leverage ceiling per regime, tightened automatically on drawdown or losing streaks.

◆

Position Sizing

Position size, leverage, and stop distance all derive from live volatility, not a static rule.

◆

Decision Logging

Every decision — including refusing to trade — is written to an auditable log with a named threshold and a stated reason.

◆

Feedback Loop

Tracks rolling win rate and drawdown per regime, surfacing threshold-adjustment suggestions over time.

Verifiable Audit Trail

Don't trust the log. Verify it.

Every row in the decision log carries a hash chain: each row's row_hash is derived from its own content plus the previous row's hash, so editing or deleting any row breaks the chain from that point forward. Each row also carries an input_hash over its exact regime-detector inputs and config — a third party can recompute the regime classification independently and confirm it matches, without trusting the log file at all.
